# Constricted Flow

*A visual philosophy*

---

Everything that moves must somewhere pass through less than it began with. This is
the first principle: abundance is not the subject — **passage** is. The work does not
depict quantity. It depicts the moment quantity is forced to become sequence, the
instant where breadth is taxed into single file and the many learn, briefly, to be
one. The eye should find this moment without being told where to look.

The form is built from **accumulation, never from statement**. A single line says
nothing; four hundred lines, each individually placed, each obeying the same quiet
law with its own small deviation, say everything. Density is the argument. The
composition must reward the long look — the viewer who stands close should find
that no two paths are identical, that every filament was given its own attention,
that the field was not generated but *drawn*. This is the difference between a
pattern and a practice. The work must carry the evidence of countless hours in its
surface, the unmistakable fingerprint of someone operating at the very top of their
craft, where restraint is the hardest discipline of all.

**Space is asymmetric and earned.** The constriction never sits at the centre of the
page; it sits where tension wants it, slightly off the true middle, so the eye falls
rather than rests. Above the waist, generosity. Below it, release — but never the
same breadth as before, because nothing passes through a narrowing and emerges
unchanged. The negative field around the form is not emptiness; it is the pressure
that makes the narrowing legible. Give it more room than feels comfortable, then a
little more.

**Colour is singular and disciplined.** One luminous value against one absorbing
ground, and nothing else permitted entry. The light must behave like metal rather
than pigment — brightest at the point of greatest compression, falling away toward
the edges, so the form appears to be lit from its own constriction rather than from
outside. Gradation does the work that a second colour would do crudely. A palette
this narrow forgives nothing: every value must be calibrated with painstaking
precision, each weight tested against its neighbours until the whole field holds
together as one surface.

**Typography is a seal, not a caption.** Words appear only where the composition has
made a place for them, set in the smallest size that remains unarguable, positioned
with the exactness of a watchmaker. They must never float, never crowd the form,
never explain what the image has already proven. One phrase carries weight; the rest
whisper at the margins as if annotating a specimen. Where type grows large, it grows
*structural* — it becomes part of the architecture rather than a label applied to it,
holding the base of the composition the way a plinth holds a sculpture.

The finished object should read as an **instrument of study** — a plate from a
discipline that does not exist, documenting a phenomenon everyone has felt and no
one has measured. Clinical in its apparatus, patient in its mark-making, and
beneath that composure, unmistakably about something human. Nothing overlaps.
Nothing touches an edge it was not meant to touch. Every alignment is the residue of
many refinements, and the whole must survive being looked at for a long time by
someone who knows exactly how hard this is to do.
