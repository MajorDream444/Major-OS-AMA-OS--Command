## Intake questions — Volume 1

Record once, roughly 45 minutes, voice is fine. Answer as a conversation, not an essay.

1. When did *you* first realise you were the bottleneck in your own business?
2. What was the moment it cost you something real — money, a relationship, your health?
3. Where were you, who was there, what was at stake?
4. What did you try first that did not work?
5. What finally changed it — a tool, a person, a decision, a failure?
6. How did the South Bronx, Mount Vernon, Jamaica, finance, athletics or travel shape
   how you see dependency and self-reliance?
7. What does conventional business advice get wrong about "work harder"?
8. What would you tell yourself five years ago about building systems?
9. Where does this principle become dangerous if overused — when does systematising cost
   something worth keeping?
10. What question should the reader carry out into their week?

### Chapter 1 — The Hidden Bottleneck
Teaching frame: most founders diagnose a time problem when they have a structure
problem. Distinguish being *busy* from being *load-bearing*.
Exercise: **Dependency Score** — list the last ten things that only you could do; score
each 1–3 on how hard it would be to hand off.
- **[INTAKE] Major's Field Note** · from Q2

### Chapter 2 — You Are the Process
Teaching frame: undocumented knowledge is not expertise, it is risk. What lives only in
your head cannot be improved, delegated, sold or inherited.
Exercise: **System Audit** — pick one repeated task, write it as steps, note where you
improvised.

### Chapter 3 — Time Is Not Scalable
Teaching frame: every growth plan priced in your hours has a ceiling you can calculate.
Make the reader calculate theirs.
Exercise: compute your real hourly capacity, then the revenue ceiling it implies.

### Chapter 4 — Build Systems, Not Heroics
Teaching frame: heroics are rewarded and unsustainable. A system that runs at 80% without
you beats a performance that runs at 100% with you.
Exercise: **Replace Yourself** — take the lowest Dependency Score from Chapter 1 and
write the handoff.
- **[INTAKE] Major's Field Note** · from Q4–Q5

### Chapter 5 — Becoming the Architect
Teaching frame: the shift from operator to architect. What you build when you are no
longer the thing being used.
- **[INTAKE] The Question I Still Carry** · from Q9
- **Try This Today:** build one AI employee that performs a single task you listed in
  the Dependency Score
