## Already answered

### Q5 — What finally changed it? ✅ strong

Puerto Rico. The decision to go and help after his mother's passing, with no plan.

> "When in doubt, help. When you don't know what to do with your life, go volunteer and
> help somebody else… I don't know what to do, but I'm big. I'm strong. Here's two hands.
> Here's two feet. Let's go."

> **"If I did not go to Puerto Rico, I would not be in tech the way that I am now."**

And the mechanism, which is the teachable part — he arrived to give, not to pitch:

> "Me just not going down there asking them for anything. But knowing that I was able to
> provide services… it was really a pivot in my life."

**Slot:** Chapter 4 Field Note (Q4–Q5).

### Q6 — How did background shape your view of dependency and self-reliance? ✅ very strong

Four independent sources, all recorded:

**Jamaica** — getting it wrong first, which is why the later work landed:
> "I had lived in Jamaica, and I knew how you have to come back to an island — you can't
> come back like you're telling them what to do with themselves, because I had gone
> through that in Jamaica and learned."

**Athletics** — being the revenue and not seeing the structure:
being *"a driver of revenue"* without sight of what is built around you. Keep the frame,
drop the figures.

**Finance** — holding a Series 7 and still not understanding the machine:
> "The money system doesn't necessarily always create value just because it has dollars
> and cents attached to it. It's just numbers on a board."

**The island as the book's own argument** — capability without self-sufficiency:
> "The knowledge has left the island… They can be super self-sustainable. But they're
> incentivised not to."

This last one is the strongest material in the archive for this specific book. The core
question of Volume 1, at the scale of a territory, witnessed rather than theorised.

**Slot:** Chapter 1 teaching frame and Chapter 5.

### Q9 — Where does this principle become dangerous if overused? ✅ strong, and unexpected

Recorded in an awareness interview, but it is precisely the Volume 1 answer — systematise
your thinking and you can stop thinking:

> "AI changes your need for awareness… it can make you not be aware. You can just take
> what your AI tells you. Now you have to actually be more aware and more discerning."

This is the honest counterweight the book needs. A book telling people to build systems
that run without them has to say where that costs something, and this says it from the
inside rather than as a disclaimer.

**Slot:** Chapter 5 — "The Question I Still Carry".

## Still missing — the short intake

Four questions. Fifteen to twenty minutes, voice is fine, conversational not essay.

**1. When did you first realise you were the bottleneck in your own business?**
Not the island, not the athlete — you. The first time you saw that the thing holding it up
was you, and that this was a problem rather than a point of pride.

**2. What did you try first that did not work?**
Before Puerto Rico, before the systems. The fix that seemed obvious and failed. This is
the question readers will recognise themselves in, because they are in the middle of
trying that same thing right now.

**3. What does conventional business advice get wrong about "work harder"?**
You have the lived refutation — the athlete who is the revenue, the island that could feed
itself. What is the argument in your own words?

**4. What would you tell yourself five years ago about building systems?**
Five years ago is the reader. Say it to them.

**Optional, if the first four come easily:** the scene around the rug pull — where you
were, who was there, what was actually at stake when it turned.
