## Q1 — The bottleneck · the San Juan dinner party

The origin scene, and the Chapter 1 field note.

A dinner party at a friend's house in Old San Juan. Twelve invited, about eighteen came.
Someone had offered to cook. He hosted.

> "It was a super beautiful affair. Like, the food was good, and at the end, like, the
> billing wasn't."

They ran out of plates. They ran out of wine. He went to the local supermarket, did not
have enough money, and bought cheaper wine —

> "which was noticed."

And the actual failure, which was not logistics:

> "It was just one of those things where my pride kicked in. So rather than charging
> people, I wanted to make it feel more like I was just hosting and being benevolent."

The lesson, stated plainly:

> "Even if people come there and you're benevolent and you're giving them stuff, people
> only remember the moment when things aren't."

**Why he was the bottleneck:** everything routed through him.

> "Rather than somebody being able to just see the situation and solve it — well, this is
> Major's thing, let me ask Major."

> "If you aren't feeling good or if you're not in the right frame of mind, you will always
> be the thing that short comes your overall growth. And it sucks when you figure out that
> it's yourself."

## The second scene — the missed cue

A play, alongside experienced actors. He missed a cue and said the wrong line — close
enough to another line that the cast followed it, and the play jumped roughly ten minutes
ahead. Five weeks earlier he had performed it flawlessly.

> "When I know that I can do something, sometimes you just end up being lazy with it.
> Sometimes you end up just being undisciplined."

A second proof of the same principle, from outside business. Useful because the reader
cannot dismiss it as a business-advice anecdote.

---

## The three root causes — Chapter 1 teaching frame

**Fear of success.**
> "If this goes right, I got a bunch more people coming… Must I have to go through all of
> this pain? Do I deserve everything that's supposed to come with success?"

**Control, earned by disappointment.** The most honest passage in the recording:
> "Working with other people has always been… disappointing in a lot of ways… there's
> friction, there's tension, and those tend to just make you say, you know what, I'll do it
> myself. I've been disappointed too much. I'll do it myself. **And you willingly become the
> bottleneck**, because as long as you know you're involved, something either gets done or
> at least it's done the way that you want it to."

**Atrophy.** The strongest image in the recording:
> "Sometimes being the bottleneck feels safe because you're slowing things down, but you
> don't realise it's really atrophy… it's actually that there's a lack of oxygen."

---

## The admission — recommended for "The Question I Still Carry"

> "That is something that ultimately I still struggle with. And I think that it's not
> really fully solved."

A book about escaping the bottleneck is more credible, not less, for saying the author has
not finished escaping it. This belongs in Chapter 5, and it is the honest counterweight
the volume needs.

## Q2 / Q4 — What he tried that did not work · the juice company

Two failures, same root cause, both his own.

**The royal event.** A cold-pressed juice and vegan café business. An opportunity to serve
at a major local event. His business partner told him not to do it.

> "I pushed and I was like, look, I'm doing it."

The audience were Emirati — a few generations from the desert, dates and camel milk.
Competing vendors sold soda and sweet juice at a fifth of the price, to families with
children. He brought roughly 300 bottles and sold perhaps 40 to 60.

> "It was things where I saw, like, *this is what you need* — versus them coming for what
> they want. A lot of the times people need certain things, but they don't want it, so you
> can't force it on them."

**The day club.** Organic cold-pressed juice into a day-drinking venue, where people mix
with cranberry, orange and tonic. Nothing organic grows in a desert, so every ingredient
was imported and the cost was unsustainable.

> "The best thing that we could have done was sell some juice that was focused on curing
> the hangover."

**The timing lesson:**
> "Sometimes the worst thing you can be is too early for a product. Or too late. Hitting
> that spot at the right time is so good."

> "Tech moves way faster than DNA. And that goes for everything — ideas, innovations, they
> move extremely fast. And DNA is the actual person, the people. They slowly come along."

**Jamaica**, same pattern, different continent: arriving to develop land with family and
discovering how political everything was.
> "Lawyers aren't really your friend. Anytime you speak to them, that bill, that phone
> call, you're getting charged for that. There were so many things I had to learn in the
> fire."

## Q7 — What "work harder" gets wrong

**The line the chapter should open on:**
> "Most people think that they own a business, but you actually own a job that you're
> shackled to."

**Where the instinct came from** — and it connects to Q6 in the earlier recording:
> "That comes from being an athlete. Going hard, running harder, jumping higher, moving
> faster — all of these types of things kind of crept over to my entrepreneurial life,
> versus measuring three times and cutting once."

**The wooden boat** — Chapter 4, *Build Systems, Not Heroics*:
> "Not really knowing that it's a wooden boat and there's holes in the boat. There were
> termites eating the wood. And with every hole, you're plugging it up, and it's just you on
> the boat — versus telling your crew, all right, plug that, plug that… You see a problem,
> you're on it. You see a problem, you're on it. And then no problem gets fixed, because
> you're on them as they pop up."

**The 80/20 passage** — the most concrete teaching in the recording, and unmistakably his
voice:
> "You wanted to be a fine dining restaurant. You realise that people are really coming at
> lunch… So you double and triple down on lunchtime takeaway. Get a smaller place, build an
> online app, create a ghost kitchen."

> "Nobody will see what you see, nobody cares what you see. The only thing people care
> about is: how does it fix my problem. I'm not coming here to solve the fact that you want
> a fine dining restaurant. I want a sandwich at lunch. **I want rice and peas and curry
> chicken at lunchtime.** That's what I want."

**And the bridge back to the Awareness recording:**
> "Know your surroundings. Always be aware. Keep your head on a swivel… You can have as
> much belief as you want, but if it's in the wrong place, it's in the wrong place."

## Q8 — What he would tell himself five years ago

> **"There's nothing like having a system. Your system is what saves you. Period. Point
> blank."**

> "Michael Jordan won because he was a great basketball player. Yes. However — the triangle
> offense."

**The recipe, which is the cleanest definition of a system in the archive:**
> "A recipe is a system. I can put one ounce of this, one ounce of this, eight ounces of
> this, and two teaspoons of this. And it will come out every time. That's what people want.
> Systems bring consistency. People bank on consistency."

**Why a system is bankable** — consistency · scalability · data · longevity:
> "If you have no system, what are you grading on? Just what you feel? You can't do that."

> "A system can bring longevity. If you put a system together, you can clone it and sell
> it. People buy systems."

**P90X** as the proof: a system sold as a system, not a product.
> "You believe when you see results."
