## The separation, and why it holds

| | **Major AI Mindset** | **MAIM Academy** |
|---|---|---|
| Is a | movement | school |
| Teaches | the ABCs — Awareness, Belief, Context | systems, implementation, one real problem |
| Asks you to | see differently | build something that runs |
| Owned by | Codex / ChatGPT | Claude |
| Outcome | followers | a cohort |

These do not drift apart, because they were never the same thing. The movement
recruits; the school delivers. Someone meets the ABCs, decides they want to
build, and arrives at the Academy. Codex can make all the ABC content it likes
without ever colliding with this channel, because the Academy's content is
defined by what it **does**, not what it believes.

**The practical test for any Academy video:** does the viewer finish it with
something to do? If not, it belongs on the Mindset channel.

## Format: long-form first, clips derived

Most channel advice says lead with shorts, because shorts grow subscribers
fastest. That advice is for building a creator business. **This channel is
filling a cohort.**

Twenty seats at US$249 does not need a big channel. It needs the right twenty.
A twelve-minute video about being the bottleneck in your own business filters
hard — everyone who finishes it has that problem and knows it. A thirty-second
clip reaches thousands of people who will never buy anything.

So: **one long-form a week, five clips out of each.** Discovery happens on the
clips, qualification happens in the long video, conversion happens on the
waitlist. This is the 1:12 ratio already written into `MAJOR_MOVES_OS.md` —
one hero asset, many derivatives.

Production is `vidiq_generate_clips` against the long video. One recording
feeds YouTube, Shorts, Instagram and Threads.

## The five

Each maps to a chapter of Volume 1, so the script already exists and is
approved. Each ends with one assignment.

### 01 · The night I found out I was the bottleneck
**Chapter 1 · ~12 min**

Opens on the Old San Juan dinner party. Eighteen people, ran out of wine, the
trip to the supermarket for the cheaper bottles — and the real failure, which
was pride, not planning.

Then the distinction that carries the series: **busy is not load-bearing.** Busy
means your day is full. Load-bearing means the thing falls down when you step
away.

Closes on the three reasons we stay there — fear of success, control earned by
disappointment, atrophy — and the admission that it is not solved.

**Assignment:** the Dependency Score. Ten things only you could do, scored 1–3.
**Clips:** the cheaper wine · "you willingly become the bottleneck" · busy vs
load-bearing · the atrophy line · "it sucks when you figure out it's yourself"

---

### 02 · What lives in your head is not expertise
**Chapter 2 · ~10 min**

Undocumented knowledge is risk, not mastery. It cannot be improved, delegated,
sold or inherited.

The spine is Major's own line: *"When you want to automate something, you have
to itemise everything that you do."* Then the uncomfortable part — itemising
exposes how much of the work is improvisation wearing the costume of expertise.
Step four is usually *"and then I look at it and I know."*

**Assignment:** the System Audit. One repeated task, written as steps, with every
improvisation marked.
**Clips:** "itemise everything you do" · step four is the one you never examined ·
"most of us do things effortlessly and don't realise not everybody can" ·
replaceable in tasks, irreplaceable in judgment · the frontier of a system

---

### 03 · Your ceiling is a number, and you can calculate it
**Chapter 3 · ~9 min**

The most concrete video of the five. Real hours, real weeks, real rate, and the
arithmetic that follows. Most people have never done this because the number is
unpleasant and vague feels better.

Three ways past the ceiling — raise the rate, add people, stop selling hours —
and only the third changes its shape.

**Assignment:** the Time Ceiling calculator. The number, the goal, the gap, and
which of the three routes you are actually taking.
**Clips:** "you're not scalable, there's not two or three of you" · the real-hours
correction · the arithmetic problem no amount of effort solves · the three routes ·
the route you assume vs the one you're on

---

### 04 · Every response correct, and the boat still sinks
**Chapter 4 · ~12 min**

The wooden boat. Termites ate the wood, every hole gets plugged, it is just you
on the boat. *You see a problem, you're on it. You see a problem, you're on it.*
And no problem ever gets fixed.

The point that makes it land: **at no stage is the founder doing anything
wrong.** Every single response is correct. The boat still sinks. That is the
difference between heroics and systems.

Then "work harder" taken apart — *"most people think they own a business, but
you actually own a job that you're shackled to"* — and where the instinct comes
from, which for Major is sport. Run harder, jump higher, versus measure three
times and cut once.

Ends on the 80/20 restaurant: the fine dining room where everyone actually comes
at lunch. *"Nobody cares what you see. I want rice and peas and curry chicken at
lunchtime."*

**Assignment:** Replace Yourself. Lowest Dependency Score, brief written, handed
over this week.
**Clips:** the wooden boat · "you own a job you're shackled to" · the athlete
instinct · the lunch realisation · "nobody cares what you see"

---

### 05 · Michael Jordan won. Also, the triangle offense.
**Chapter 5 · ~11 min**

*"Your system is what saves you. Period, point blank."*

A recipe is a system — one ounce of this, two teaspoons of that, and it comes out
the same every time. Four things a system gives you that effort never will:
predictability, scale, data, longevity. P90X sold a sequence with a promise
attached, and the sequence was the product.

Then the honest limit, which is why this video is last: systematise your hands
and you get free; systematise your **thinking** and you stop thinking.
*"AI changes your need for awareness. It can make you not be aware."*

Ends on the question Major actually asks: **what blind spots are we not looking
at? What am I missing?**

**Assignment:** build one AI employee for a single task off the Dependency Score.
Watch it run end to end before trusting it.
**Clips:** "your system is what saves you" · the recipe · Jordan and the triangle ·
"if someone wanted to buy what you do, what would you hand them?" · AI can make
you less aware

## Production

**Source footage.** `the mindset of major pt1.mp4` (278 MB) and
`Brother Ling Conversation.mp4` (387 MB) are in Drive and are real recordings.
The three Fathom intakes are **audio only** — usable as voice over the
Constricted Flow field, not as talking head.

Some Drive videos are Gemini-generated and carry a synthesised likeness. **Do not
use those.** The design lock forbids a synthesised likeness, and the garbled
on-screen text in at least one of them would undercut a channel whose whole
pitch is being straight with people.

**Per video:** record or pull long-form → `vidiq_video_transcript` →
`vidiq_generate_clips` → thumbnail via `maim-brand-production` →
`vidiq_score_title` before committing to a title → upload →
clips scheduled through Metricool to Instagram, Threads and Shorts.

**Thumbnails** follow the Constricted Flow language, 1280×720, built with the
same tokens as the covers so the channel and the book read as one thing.

**Cadence.** One long-form a week is the plan, not a promise. Do not publish a
schedule to the audience until two videos exist, and never claim a cadence the
channel has not yet kept.

## CTA discipline

majoraimindset.com.**

comment or end screen may imply a seat can be bought. When enrollment genuinely
opens, the CTA changes in one pass across all five.
