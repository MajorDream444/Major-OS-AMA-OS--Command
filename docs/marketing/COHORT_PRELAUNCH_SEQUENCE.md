# Founding cohort — pre-launch email sequence

**Status:** draft for Major's approval. Nothing is scheduled or sent.
**Trigger:** ticks the cohort box on majoraimindset.com
**Segment:** `MAIM · Founding Cohort Waitlist` — `d2c9ec40-8e95-4aad-92b2-5a098abb03c2`
**Goal:** arrive at opening day with a warm room instead of an announcement into silence.
**Length:** 5 emails before the door opens, 1 on the day.
**Exit:** enrolls, or unsubscribes.

## The constraint this is written under

Enrollment is closed. Live sales are disabled. Dates are planned, not confirmed.

So this sequence sells nothing. It gives away the book's best thinking, in Major's
own recorded words, and tells the truth about where the door is. Every line below
survives the test: *if the date moved tomorrow, would this email still be honest?*

No countdown pressure, no seats-remaining number, no "last chance". Those are
forbidden by the cohort document and they would spend trust the book spent 28 pages
earning.

## Email 2 · The dinner party
**Send:** day 2

**Subject:** The night I found out I was the problem
**Preview:** Old San Juan, eighteen people, and a trip to the supermarket for cheaper wine.

> I never thought I was the bottleneck in my own business. These were my ideas.
>
> Then I threw a dinner party in Old San Juan. Invited about twelve people, eighteen
> came. Someone offered to cook. It was a beautiful affair, the food was good, and at
> the end the billing wasn't.
>
> We ran out of plates. We ran out of wine. I went to the local supermarket and I
> didn't have enough money, so I bought a bunch of cheaper wine. It was noticed.
>
> Here's the part that took me longer to see. The problem wasn't the planning. My
> pride kicked in. Rather than charging people, I wanted it to feel like I was just
> hosting, being benevolent. None of those people were broke. I just didn't want to
> be the guy asking.
>
> Even when you're being generous, people only remember the moment when things
> aren't right. That's what sticks.
>
> And everything in that room ran through me. Nobody could look at a situation and
> solve it, because the answer was always *this is Major's thing, let me ask Major.*
>
> That's a bottleneck. The neck of a bottle is the slimmest part. When it gets there,
> everything slows down.
>
> Look at your list from the other day. How many of those threes are there because
> nobody else is allowed to decide?
>
> Major

**CTA:** none. This one just lands.

## Email 3 · Why it feels like competence
**Send:** day 5

**Subject:** Busy is not the same as load-bearing
**Preview:** One of these means your day is full. The other means it falls down without you.

> There's a difference between being busy and being load-bearing.
>
> Busy means your day is full. Load-bearing means the whole thing falls down if you
> step away. You can be busy and completely replaceable. You can also be load-bearing
> and look, from outside, like you're barely doing anything, right up until the week
> you get sick.
>
> The cruel part is that it feels like competence. People come to you because you're
> good. You answer because you're fast. Every time you solve something personally you
> prove you're the right person to solve it, and the opening gets narrower.
>
> Three reasons we stay there, and none of them are laziness.
>
> Fear of success. If this works, more people come, and more of everything you're not
> sure you can carry.
>
> Control, earned honestly. You handed something over and it came back wrong. So you
> took it back. That's a scar, not a character flaw. But a scar that makes you do
> everything yourself costs you everything you can't build.
>
> Atrophy. Nothing is going wrong. You're just the only source of oxygen in the room,
> and the room is getting smaller.
>
> I'll tell you plainly: I still struggle with this. It isn't solved. I'm not writing
> from the other side.
>
> Major

## Email 4 · The wooden boat
**Send:** day 9

**Subject:** Work harder is the wrong instruction
**Preview:** Every response correct, and the boat still sinks.

> Most people think they own a business. You actually own a job that you're shackled
> to.
>
> Here's the picture I keep coming back to. You don't realise it's a wooden boat and
> there are holes in it. Termites ate the wood. Every hole, you're plugging it up, and
> it's just you on the boat, because this is your baby.
>
> You see a problem, you're on it. You see a problem, you're on it. You see a problem,
> you're on it. And no problem ever gets fixed, because you're on them as they pop up.
> Before you know it there are too many holes.
>
> Read that again and notice what's missing. At no point is the founder doing anything
> wrong. He sees a problem and he fixes it. Every single response is correct.
>
> The boat still sinks.
>
> That's the difference between heroics and systems. Heroics are a sequence of correct
> responses to symptoms. A system asks what's eating the wood.
>
> Mine came from sport. Run harder, jump higher, move faster. All of that crept into
> my business life, instead of measuring three times and cutting once.
>
> Major

>
> So ask the uncomfortable version about your own business. If someone wanted to buy

## Notes for sending

- Emails 2–5 carry **no CTA on purpose.** Five emails of asking trains people to
  stop opening. These give; email 6 asks once.
- Every quoted passage is Major's own, from the 14 and 23 October intake recordings.
  Nothing here invents biography.
- Email 6 is written against the inclusions in `FOUNDING-OFFER.md` and
  `FOUNDING-COHORT-2026-10-21.md`. **Do not send it until checkout, access and the
  seat cap are tested** — the cohort document lists those as open gates.
- Day counts are from signup, not from a fixed calendar, so the sequence works for
  someone who joins the list late.
- If the first session date moves, emails 1–5 remain true. That was the design
  constraint.

## Measure

Open rate per email, and which one loses people. Reply rate on email 2 — the dinner
party is the one that earns replies, and a reply from a waitlister is worth more
than a click. Then waitlist-to-enrollment on email 6.
