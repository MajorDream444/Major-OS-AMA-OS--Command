## Why it is worth cataloguing

Every one of these is Major talking about the businesses he is actually in. Not
positioning, not a case study — the thing itself, as it happened. That is the
one asset a competitor cannot assemble, and the one AI cannot generate.

Three jobs it can do, in order of how soon:

1. **Clips.** Short proof that a real person has been saying this for years.
   Feeds the Mindset lane without consuming a single Academy recording slot.
2. **Evidence.** When the book says a thing, the archive shows him saying it
   before there was a book to sell.
3. **The vault.** The token-gated tier. This is the part worth being deliberate
   about — see below.

## On the vault

Major's instinct, stated 24 Oct: *maybe this is also why I'm building the vault.*

The reasoning holds. Gated access works when what is behind the gate cannot be
got elsewhere and does not expire. A course expires the moment someone writes a
better one. A decade of a founder thinking out loud, in rooms, while building —
that does not. It only gets more valuable as the businesses it describes mature.

What it is **not**, and this matters: it is not a tier of the Academy. The
Academy sells an outcome — you build something that runs. The vault sells
access. Mixing them makes the Academy look like a content library and makes the
vault look like a bonus. Separate products, separate promises.

**Nothing here is active.** No vault exists, no gate is built, no token is
issued, nothing is priced. This records the thinking so the archive is not
treated as spare footage before the decision is made.
