## The definition — usable as the opening of A · Awareness

> "Awareness is being outside of yourself, watching yourself as if you're watching a
> movie."

Extended in his words: tracking physical, geopolitical and technological surroundings;
how the body feels on waking; traversing your own timeline — how you felt last week, how
you felt yesterday, how you project forward — and grading it along the way.

> "It's very hard to have self-awareness because you inhabit a body and you're always
> looking outside into the world and dealing with things as they come."

This is why systems and coaches exist. Strong teaching spine for the chapter.

---

## The closing line — the thesis of the whole letter

> "Your awareness is your perspective. The more perspective you have, the more awareness
> you have."

Preceded by the six-story building image: people on the sixth floor have awareness
because they see over everyone; people on the first floor have awareness because they see
everyone.

> "I do hope that people get a chance to live on all of those floors, because it's only at
> that point you'll realise that we all kind of have the same problems, no matter what
> floor you live on."

**Recommended as the closing manifesto.** It lands the cultural and class argument without
stating it.

## Field notes — candidates for "Major's Field Note"

**1. His mother.** ⚠️ *Sensitive — explicit approval required.* After she passed, he moved
and was still for the first time in years. The realisation: he had never seen her as a
woman, only as his mother — her sacrifices, what she gave up, having him young and raising
two sons after his father died when he was five. *"The grace that I held back on giving
her."* The strongest and most personal material in the recording.

**2. The money system.** He held a Series 7 and still did not understand that a bank is a
business lending against interest, or what fractional banking and the debt trap were.
> "The money system doesn't necessarily always create value just because it has dollars
> and cents attached to it. It's just numbers on a board."
Excellent for a business readership — expertise and blindness coexisting.

**3. The athlete as commodity.** On the tax asymmetry between an employed athlete and an
entrepreneur earning the same, and being *"a driver of revenue"* without seeing the
structure around you. Keep the comparison, drop specific figures.

**4. The rug pull.** Two years of winning in an early-stage tech market, then the reversal.
> "As soon as things start going good, you quickly forget about that pain until it comes
> back around again."
And the lesson underneath: *"The goal to make money is to let the next person think
there's still money there to make."*

**5. Culture shock.** Not international — a move from a major city to a small rural town.
> "Everyone's just trying to have a good life… there's more things that bind us than
> differentiate."

## On AI — directly usable, and it is the MAIM argument

> "AI changes your need for awareness… it can make you not be aware. You can just take
> what your AI tells you. Now you have to actually be more aware and more discerning."

On why AI feedback lands where human feedback does not: you read it as indifferent and
constructive, so you can let it rip your ideas apart without defending yourself.

His own habitual prompt, which belongs in the book as a Major Move:
> "What blind spots are we not looking at? What am I missing?"

On automation: *"When you want to automate something, you have to itemise everything that
you do."* — this is **Volume 1 of Major Moves**, arriving unprompted in an awareness
interview.

## For the younger-learner editions

Unprompted, he gave child-appropriate awareness teaching:

- Friends versus people you are friendly with: *"Kids meet each other one day — oh, you're
  my friend. No, that's somebody you're friendly with. It takes time to become friends."*
- Learn a combat sport, because discomfort teaches you that discomfort passes:
  *"You kind of learn to be comfortable with being uncomfortable."*
- Life has ebb and flow; you will be uncomfortable again, and that is fine.

Maps cleanly to **The Truth Compass** (7–12) and **The Integrity Code** (13–17).
