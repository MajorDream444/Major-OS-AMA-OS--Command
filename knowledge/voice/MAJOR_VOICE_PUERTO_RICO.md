## The passage that matters most — the career pivot

This is the strongest narrative in the recording and it belongs in a business book, not
a travel chapter. In his words, compressed in sequence:

He went after his mother's health decline and passing, not knowing his next move. A
hurricane hit. He flew down to help.

> "When in doubt, help. When you don't know what to do with your life, go volunteer and
> help somebody else… I don't know what to do, but I'm big. I'm strong. Here's two hands.
> Here's two feet. Let's go."

The recovery effort put him among people who had relocated for the island's tax status —
crypto, gold, real estate, law.

> "So now I'm sitting down there with all of these wealthy people that I would never have
> access to in the real world."

And the hinge:

> **"If I did not go to Puerto Rico, I would not be in tech the way that I am now."**

What made it work was not asking:

> "Me just not going down there asking them for anything. But knowing that I was able to
> provide services… it was really a pivot in my life, a strong pivot in my life."

**Why this is a business lesson, not an anecdote:** he arrived to give, not to pitch, and
the access followed. That is a teachable principle with a lived proof behind it — rare,
and exactly what the Major Moves volumes need.

## The credential behind the cultural work

> "I had lived in Jamaica, and I knew how you have to come back to an island — you can't
> come back like you're telling them what to do with themselves, because I had gone
> through that in Jamaica and learned."

He became the bridge between relocated wealth and the island because he had already got
it wrong once somewhere else. **This is the origin of the MAIM diaspora position**, stated
plainly and without self-congratulation. Pillar 01 material.

## Observations usable as teaching

**Three competing cultures.** "They are Spanish. They are Caribbean. They are American."
Plus the ability to leave — so there is a brain drain.

> "There's a level of inefficiency that happens… that they enjoy, because in inefficiency,
> there's a lot of opportunity."

**The sovereignty argument, made concretely.** Nothing ships direct; goods route through a
mainland port first. Roughly three weeks of food storage. A Caribbean island that can grow
everything and exports little.

> "The knowledge has left the island… They can be super self-sustainable. But they're
> incentivised not to."

This is the clearest statement of the MAIM thesis anywhere in the archive — capability
present, structure preventing it. **Strong candidate for a Ten Pillars opening.**

**Promises and outsiders.** Why trust is withheld: a century of being promised things.

> "It's the typical thing when you go to an island — you fall in love with somebody, you
> say I'm coming back, and you never come back."

**Who actually holds things together.**
> "Everything operates with the women and through the women. The food, the music, the art."

## Place writing — for the Lucky Library

The walled city: blue brick streets that read grey from a distance, Spanish colonial
facades protected so all construction happens inside, the fort and the open green where
families fly kites at the weekend.

The neighbourhood at the Atlantic edge: its own rules and governance, no recording, lively
with music and food, exposed to high tide and hurricane. Recorded with respect and without
romanticising.

The shift across the island: salsa and merengue in the capital, bomba further along —
"more African-led" — and the moment of arriving somewhere that finally feels like an
island rather than a city.

> "You can live in Old San Juan and not feel like you're on an island. But in Luisa, you
> definitely feel you're on an island."

The interior: cowboy culture, horses that skip rather than gallop, men racing them at
night with sparks coming off the shoes.
> "I'd never seen anything like this. That really blew my mind."

Seventy-eight municipalities on an island a hundred kilometres across, each with its own
way of being — his own framing for why a place cannot be generalised.

**The before and after.** He had visited as a schoolboy to play basketball, then returned
after the hurricane to a changed island.
> "I felt very, very cheated by, like I wish I would have really experienced Puerto Rico
> before."
And after the earthquakes, people sleeping under highways rather than inside old buildings.

## Character study — kept as a role, name withheld

An innkeeper in her late seventies: married young into wealth, widowed, remarried more
than once, inherited a collection of historic buildings woven into one hotel, left in
serious debt by a husband who was poor with money, and who then made it profitable again
herself.

A resilience story with a woman at the centre — thematically aligned with the Pillars and
usable with her permission. **Do not publish identifying detail without it.**
